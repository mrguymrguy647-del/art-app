# Van Gogh, The Starry Night

**Shapes.** A sky of swirls filling the top two thirds; a dark cypress as a flame shape on the
left, cutting the sky from bottom to top; a village of small dark boxes along the bottom with
a pale church spire; hills rolling between them; eleven stars and a crescent moon as yellow
rings.

**Values** (`van-gogh-starry-night-values.png`: black 27%, dark 32%, mid 28%, light 12%).
Four groups when squinted, darks dominant, lights kept for the stars and the moon. Squinted,
the cypress is the darkest shape and the moon the lightest, and they sit at opposite corners.

**Palette** (`van-gogh-starry-night-palette.png`): near-black, three blues from deep to pale,
grey-greens, and one yellow. Blue against yellow is the whole colour idea.

**Why it works.** Every mark is a stroke that follows a direction (the swirls, the cypress's
flames, the hills' curves): texture as *marks*, never as grain. The critique should count the
sky as detailed, not noisy, and it does. The complementary pair blue/yellow is used with one
side dominant (blue over 80% of the picture) and the other as accents.

**Study.** `van-gogh-starry-night-study.json` blocks in the big shapes with the palette above (about twenty commands, no detail). `art-cli compare van-gogh-starry-night-study.json van-gogh-starry-night.*`: values similar: 72; colours somewhat alike: 54. A study that scores 70 or more on values has the picture's value structure; the colour score says whether the palette is in the same key, not whether the painting is any good.
