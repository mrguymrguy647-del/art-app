# Chelsea the cat

**Shapes.** One face filling the frame, soft at the edges where the fur goes out of focus; two
green eyes with black slits as the only hard, high-contrast shapes; a pink nose below them.

**Values** (`chelsea-values.png`: dark 25%, mid 58%, light 13%). Mostly mid: the critique would
call a painting like this nearly flat, and that's the lesson. What saves it is that the few
lights (the cheeks, the eye highlights) and darks (the stripes, the pupils) are placed around
the eyes, so the focal point has contrast even though the whole has little.

**Palette** (`chelsea-palette.png`): eight browns and tans, one hue in eight values. The eyes'
green doesn't even make the top eight: a tiny area of a complementary colour at the focal point.

**Why it works.** Texture reads as *marks* (stripes, whiskers, fur direction), never as noise:
every mark follows the form. A painting of fur wants a few hundred directional strokes, not a
noise fill.

**Study.** `chelsea-study.json` blocks in the big shapes with the palette above (about twenty commands, no detail). `art-cli compare chelsea-study.json chelsea.*`: values somewhat alike: 66; colours similar: 89. A study that scores 70 or more on values has the picture's value structure; the colour score says whether the palette is in the same key, not whether the painting is any good.
