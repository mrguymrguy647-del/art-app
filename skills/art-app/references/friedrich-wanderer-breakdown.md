# Friedrich, Wanderer above the Sea of Fog

**Shapes.** A dark figure on a dark rock, dead centre, seen from behind; a sea of pale fog
filling the middle; grey mountain ridges rising out of it on both sides; a pale sky above.

**Values** (`friedrich-wanderer-values.png`: black 26%, dark 5%, mid 11%, light 43%, white
15%). Five groups, and almost nothing in between the black figure and the light fog: the
picture is two values with a soft grey middle distance. The figure reads at any size because
it is the darkest shape against the lightest field.

**Palette** (`friedrich-wanderer-palette.png`): pale blue-greys, mid greys, near-black. One
hue, hardly saturated: the whole picture is drawn in value.

**Why it works.** Dead centre is a composition rule the critique warns about, and this
painting breaks it on purpose: the figure is centred so the viewer stands where he stands.
A warning is a reason to look, not a rule to obey. The fog is low in structure *as a field*,
and that is right for fog; it would be a finding on a cloud that needs a lit side.
