# Monet, Impression, Sunrise

**Shapes.** A grey-blue fog over everything; the harbour's cranes and masts as faint vertical
smudges; two dark boats low in the middle; one small orange disc (the sun) high right of
centre, with its broken reflection as a few orange dashes down the water.

**Values** (`monet-impression-sunrise-values.png`: dark 23%, mid 76%). Two value groups when
squinted. By the critique's rule this picture is nearly flat, and that is the famous point of
it: almost everything sits in one narrow band of mid grey-blue, so the one small sun, which is
*no lighter* than the sky in value, carries the whole picture by hue and saturation alone.
A known false alarm for the numbers: the critique can call this "flat" and be wrong.

**Palette** (`monet-impression-sunrise-palette.png`): eight greys that lean blue-green, one
warm grey; the sun's orange is too small an area to make the top eight.

**Why it works.** One temperature over the whole canvas, broken by a single warm accent at the
focal point; big soft shapes and a few quick dark marks (the boats, the reflection) that read
as marks, not noise. If a picture of yours scores "flat", ask whether it has this picture's
one unmissable accent. Most don't.

**Study.** `monet-impression-sunrise-study.json` blocks in the big shapes with the palette above (about twenty commands, no detail). `quill compare monet-impression-sunrise-study.json monet-impression-sunrise.*`: values somewhat alike: 66; colours somewhat alike: 52, the study is warmer. A study that scores 70 or more on values has the picture's value structure; the colour score says whether the palette is in the same key, not whether the painting is any good.
