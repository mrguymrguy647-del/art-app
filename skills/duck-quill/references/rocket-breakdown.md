# Falcon 9 on the pad at dusk

**Shapes.** A dark blue sky gradient over most of the frame; a thin white vertical (the rocket)
dead centre, flanked by two dark towers and two more at the edges; a row of warm lights along
the bottom.

**Values** (`rocket-values.png`: black 43%, dark 50%, mid 5%, light 1%, white 1%). Almost all
dark: three groups when squinted. The critique's "flat" rule doesn't fire because the dark
bins aren't the middle ones, and the picture isn't flat: the 2% of light pixels are the subject.

**Palette** (`rocket-palette.png`): six blues and greys, then two small warm colours (the
lights). Cool key, warm accents: the oldest trick, and the eye goes to the warm lights and the
lit rocket at once.

**Why it works.** Symmetry made deliberate (the rocket is centred, the towers pair up), a big
calm field of one dark colour, and the lights at the bottom as the only saturated warm notes.
For a night painting: keep 90% of the canvas in two dark values and spend the light sparingly.

**Study.** `rocket-study.json` blocks in the big shapes with the palette above (about twenty commands, no detail). `quill compare rocket-study.json rocket.*`: values similar: 84; colours similar: 91. A study that scores 70 or more on values has the picture's value structure; the colour score says whether the palette is in the same key, not whether the painting is any good.
