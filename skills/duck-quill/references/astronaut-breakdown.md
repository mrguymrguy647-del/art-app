# Astronaut: official portrait

**Shapes.** One big warm shape (the orange suit) filling the lower two thirds; a light oval (the
face and hair) above its centre; a black ring (the helmet collar) between them; a dark shape
with a white model shuttle on the right, the flag's stripes on the left.

**Values** (`astronaut-values.png`: black 29%, dark 12%, mid 23%, light 26%, white 11%). Five
groups, well spread: the picture reads at any size. The lightest values sit on the face and
the shuttle, the darkest around the face (collar, visor), so the face has the strongest
contrast in the picture and the eye goes there first.

**Palette** (`astronaut-palette.png`): a pale warm grey (the background and the face's lights),
the orange of the suit, black, then mid greys and skin tones, a deep red for the suit's shade.
Two hues carry almost everything: orange against the cool grey of the background.

**Why it works.** A studio portrait's rules: the face is the lightest warm shape against a
darker ring, the suit is one flat saturated mass that frames it, and everything else is low in
saturation. A painting of a character can use the same three-part stack: dark ring, light
face, one big colour below.

**Study.** `astronaut-study.json` blocks in the big shapes with the palette above (about twenty commands, no detail). `quill compare astronaut-study.json astronaut.*`: values similar: 76; colours somewhat alike: 42. A study that scores 70 or more on values has the picture's value structure; the colour score says whether the palette is in the same key, not whether the painting is any good.
