# A value study in three greys

## Do

Paint a landscape with a palette of exactly three greys (plus the paper): sky, far hills,
near ground, and one thing at the focal point. No colour, no gradients inside a shape bigger
than the sun, no texture. 640 x 360. Write the intent first, with `focal` naming the thing.

The point: decide the three to five big value shapes before anything else, and put the
strongest contrast where the eye should go.

## What a good result looks like

`value-study.png` is one answer: a pale sky, a mid ridge, a dark near ridge, a dark tree on it
with a white sun behind. `quill critique value-study.json` says:

- `values: 4 value groups when squinted (dark 30%, mid 27%, light 36%, white 6%)`: three or
  more groups with real area is the goal; one or two means the shapes are not separated.
- `focal contrast: the focal point has the strongest contrast in the picture (1.8x the next
  strongest, at sun)`: the tree against the sun is the strongest edge, and the eye goes there.
- `focal point: the eye goes to tree; inside the focal part`.
- `readability at 96 px: 3 of 4 parts still read`; the far hills merge with the sky (value
  gap 0.07): that is the one thing to fix in this answer, by darkening the far hills a step.
- Expected and fine here: every part is `flat` and a `smudge` (no change in value inside it).
  A value study is flat fills by design; in a finished painting the same lines would be
  findings.
