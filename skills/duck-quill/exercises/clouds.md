# Five kinds of clouds

## Do

On one sky gradient, paint five clouds in a row and label them: a cumulus (a lit top, a dark
flat underside, a crisp side towards the light), a stratus (long flat bands, soft), a cirrus
(thin wisps with a chalk brush), a storm front (a dark mass with a light rim where the sun
catches its edge) and a sunset cloud (a warm top, a purple underside). 960 x 400. Make the
cumulus the focal point.

The point: every cloud that is not a wisp has a lit side and a shadow side, and at least one
crisp edge; a cloud with neither is a smudge (the sunset pond's clouds in `../mistakes.md`).

## What a good result looks like

`clouds.png` is one answer; it is deliberately simple (flat shapes, no noise), so the critique
reports what flat shapes report:

- `out of key: nothing` and `readability at 96 px: 4 of 4 parts still read`: every cloud
  reads as a silhouette.
- No part is `blotchy` and no edge is `soft`: the shapes have crisp edges and a direction.
- Expected and fine here: the parts are `flat` inside (flat fills by design); `the eye goes
  to storm`, not the cumulus: the storm front is the darkest shape on a light sky, so the eye
  goes there first. In a real sky the painter would either make the storm the focal point or
  give the cumulus the strongest edge.

What would be wrong: a `blotchy` cloud (a noise fill with no lit side), a `smudge`, or an
`edge is soft and the lighter background shows through` on a cumulus (a blur on the whole
shape).
